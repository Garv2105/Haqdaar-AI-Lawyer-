**Short answer** 
The Model Tenancy Act says a landlord must refund the security deposit when he takes back vacant possession, after deducting only what the tenant actually owes [1]. It does not prescribe a fixed 18 % interest rate, so the law on that point is unclear.

**What you can do – from easiest to hardest**

1. **Send a written demand** – Email, WhatsApp or a legal‑notice‑style letter (Speed Post with tracking). State the amount (Rs 50,000), ask for the deposit back, and give a 15‑day deadline. Keep the proof; it often settles the dispute and will be useful later [2]. 
 *Cost*: free (post fee only). 
 *Time*: a few days to draft and send.

2. **Try a Lok Adalat** – If the landlord agrees, you can go to a Lok Adalat, a settlement forum where the decision is final and binding [3]. 
 *Cost*: usually free or very low. 
 *Time*: weeks to a few months, depending on the centre.

3. **File a summary suit under CPC Order XXXVII** – If you have a written rent agreement, you can file a fast‑track civil suit for the fixed sum of the deposit. The court may give the landlord “leave to defend” only after seeing the contract [4]. 
 *Cost*: court fees (varies) and lawyer’s fee. 
 *Time*: a few months to a year.

4. **Regular civil suit** – If a summary suit is not possible, you can file a normal suit for recovery of money. The limitation period is three years from when the right to sue arose [5]. Since the deposit has been withheld for 8 months, you are well within the limit. 
 *Cost*: higher court fees and lawyer’s fee. 
 *Time*: 1‑2 years on average.

**When to see a lawyer**

Because the interest question is unclear and you may need to draft a legal notice or decide which court to approach, a lawyer can help. If your income is low, you may qualify for free legal aid – call the NALSA helpline 15100 or approach the District Legal Services Authority [6].

**Bottom line** – You are likely entitled to get the Rs 50,000 back, but the law does not specify a 18 % interest. Start with a written demand; if that fails, consider Lok Adalat or a summary suit. If you need help with the interest issue or the filing, get a lawyer’s advice.
