**Short answer** 
Yes, the law is on your side. Under the Code on Wages, monthly wages must be paid before the 7th day of the next month, and if you resign the dues must be cleared within 2 working days [1]. The startup has already missed those deadlines for September and October.

**What you can do – from easiest to hardest**

1. **Send a written demand** – Email or WhatsApp a dated note (or a Speed‑Post legal notice) stating: you left on 15 Oct 2026, salary of Rs 45,000 × 2 is owed, and you need payment within 15 days. Keep the sent copy as proof. This often settles the matter [2]. 
 *Cost*: free (post‑age). 
 *Time*: a few days to draft and send.

2. **File a wage claim with the labour authority** – Approach the district labour office (Uttar Pradesh labour department). You, a registered union, or an inspector can file. The claim must be lodged within 3 years of the right to sue, and the authority should decide within 3 months [3]. 
 *Cost*: usually nominal filing fee; you can ask the office. 
 *Time*: a few weeks to prepare, then up to 3 months for a decision. 
 *What you need*: offer letter, payslips up to Aug 2026, HR emails, any salary slips or bank statements for Sep‑Oct (if you can get them), and the written demand you sent.

3. **Approach the free legal‑aid helpline** – Call 15100 or visit NALSA’s portal for advice and possibly a lawyer’s help at no charge. They can guide you on the exact forms and state‑specific steps [4] (the Code on Wages applies nationwide after 21 Nov 2025). 

4. **Civil suit for recovery of money** – If the labour route fails, you can sue the company in a civil court. The suit must be filed within 3 years from when the right to sue arose (the missed‑payment date) [5]. This is more costly and time‑consuming, so use it as a last resort.

**Deadlines to watch** 
- Salary for September was due by 7 Nov 2026; October salary by 7 Nov 2026. 
- You have up to 3 years from those dates to file a claim [5].

**What’s missing** 
- Exact due dates (usually the 7th of the next month). 
- Any written notice from the startup about the delay. 

**When to see a lawyer** 
If you need a formal legal notice, or the labour authority’s decision is unsatisfactory, a lawyer can help draft the notice and represent you. You can get free assistance through 15100.

I’m not a lawyer, so for any step that feels tricky, please confirm with a qualified lawyer or the legal‑aid helpline.

- [4] Code on Wages, 2019, Section 69 - Old wage laws repealed from 21 November 2025
