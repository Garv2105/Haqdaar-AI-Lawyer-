**Short answer** 
Yes, the law is likely on your side. The Code on Wages (which replaced the Payment of Wages Act on 21 Nov 2025) still protects wages that were due after that date, and it requires monthly wages to be paid before the 7th day of the next month [1][2].

**What you can do – from easiest to hardest**

1. **Send a written demand** – Email or WhatsApp a dated message that states: you have not been paid since August 2026, the amount you claim, and you give a 15‑day deadline to pay. Keep the sent‑message screenshot as proof. This often makes the employer pay and also creates evidence if you later go to a forum [3].

2. **File a wage claim with the labour authority** – Go to the district labour office (or the online portal if your state has one) and lodge a claim for unpaid wages. The authority must decide within about 3 months and you have up to 3 years from the date the wages became due to file [4]. You will need any proof you can gather (pay slips, bank statements, emails, the demand you sent, etc.) [5].

3. **Start a summary suit in civil court** – If you have a written employment contract and the amount is a fixed sum, you can ask a lawyer to file a summary suit under CPC Order XXXVII. The court will let the employer defend only with permission. This route is faster than a regular suit but needs a written contract and a lawyer [6].

**Key deadlines** 
- Wage claim must be filed within 3 years of the unpaid salary becoming due [4]. 
- The employer should have paid by the 7th day of the next month [2].

**What I’m not sure about** 
- Whether the unpaid salary falls under the old Payment of Wages Act or the new Code on Wages (a lawyer can confirm). 
- The exact amount you are owed, any written notice you may have already sent, and any earlier attempts you made to recover the money.

**When to see a lawyer** 
Because you need to confirm which law applies and may need help preparing the labour‑authority claim or a summary suit, it’s wise to talk to a lawyer. You can also call the free legal‑aid helpline 15100 for advice. 

Good luck, and keep all your communications and documents dated and saved.
