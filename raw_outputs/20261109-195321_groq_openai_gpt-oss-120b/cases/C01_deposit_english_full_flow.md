> I moved out of my 1BHK in Koramangala, Bengaluru on 31 August 2026. I paid Rs 60,000 as security deposit when I moved in (rent was Rs 30,000). The landlord Mr. Ramesh Gowda is now saying he will keep Rs 35,000 for painting and 'general wear and tear'. He hasn't shown any bills. I have the rent agreement and the UPI receipt for the deposit.

**Haqdaar** (topic: deposit, mode: explain, language: en, needs lawyer: False, 4963 ms):

**Short answer** 
The law says a landlord can keep only the amount that is actually owed for damage, and must show proof of the cost. Since he has not given any bills or notice, the deposit should be returned to you. [1]

---

### What you can do (easy → harder)

1. **Send a written demand** – Email or WhatsApp a dated note saying: you moved out on 31 Aug 2026, you paid Rs 60,000, the landlord is withholding Rs 35,000 without proof, and you want the full amount back within 15 days. Keep the sent‑message screenshot. This often settles the matter. [2]

2. **Collect and organise evidence** – Keep the rent agreement, UPI receipt, any chat messages, photos of the flat at move‑out, and a timeline of events. This will help any later step. [3]

3. **Call free legal‑aid helpline** – Dial 15100 (NALSA) or visit your District Legal Services Authority. If your income is low you may get a free lawyer. [4]

4. **File a summary suit for the deposit** – Because you have a written rent agreement, you can approach a civil court under CPC Order XXXVII for a quick money suit. You’ll need the agreement, payment proof, and the demand letter. A lawyer will draft the petition. [5]

5. **Try a Lok Adalat** – If both you and the landlord agree, the case can be taken to a Lok Adalat, whose settlement is final and binding. No appeal is allowed. [6]

---

### Time limit 
A suit to recover money must be filed within 3 years from the day the right to sue arose. Here that date is roughly 31 Aug 2026, so you have time, but don’t wait too long. [7]

---

### What’s still unclear 

* **Whether Karnataka has adopted the Model Tenancy Act** – the rule above applies only in states that have enacted it. You may need to confirm this. 
* **Any clause in your agreement about wear‑and‑tear deductions** – check the contract for such a clause. 

If you are unsure about the state law or need help drafting the demand or suit, a lawyer (or free legal aid) can guide you. 

Good luck, and keep all your documents handy!
